# How Granola gets customers

**One-line verdict:** Likely an individual note-taking win creates useful shared outputs, which expose colleagues to the product and open a path to team context and paid integrations. [E1, E2, E4, E5, E6]

## TL;DR

- Granola makes the initial promise concrete: better meeting notes without a meeting bot, with private notes and optional sharing. Likely this addresses an obvious first-use objection. [E1]
- Its 2.0 announcement makes shared folders and shareable team context explicit; the later announcement expands that into Spaces and APIs. These are product distribution surfaces, not measured referral attribution. [E5, E6]
- The pricing page keeps a free entry and distinguishes paid history, integrations and administration. Likely expansion follows ongoing usefulness and organizational needs. [E2]

## The engine

Likely the loop is capture one meeting → get a useful note → share a result with a colleague → create a shared body of context → expand access and integrations. The homepage supplies the individual promise, the team announcement supplies collaboration, and the later Spaces/API announcement connects the archive to other workflows. This is a mechanism inferred from product design, not a proven referral conversion rate. [E1, E5, E6]

## Steal this playbook

Your playbook is ranked by expected impact per effort; counts below are proposed tests, not Granola performance benchmarks. [E11]

1. **Publish one before-and-after meeting brief.** First step: use an invented sample meeting clearly labeled as such and show editable decisions and next actions. Watch sample view → completed first brief. [E1, E3]
2. **Build controlled output sharing.** First step: let the creator preview exactly what a recipient can see before sharing. Watch shared briefs, recipient views and recipient activations, without exposing private meeting content. [E1, E5]
3. **Publish three job-specific guides.** Start with discovery call follow-up, project handoff and weekly client decision recap. Watch nonbrand impressions and completed briefs from each guide. [E7, E9]
4. **Make a one-week habit experiment.** First step: prompt early users to evaluate three relevant meetings and review the useful actions retained. Watch second-week repeat use and correction rate. Likely repeat value is the prerequisite for a sharing loop. [E1, E4]
5. **Offer one practical export before many integrations.** First step: format a reviewed brief for the consulting team's existing document workflow. Watch exports that lead to repeated use. [E2, E6]
6. **Gate team expansion on real collaboration.** First step: add one shared client-project folder once users need it and explain access clearly. Watch colleague invitations and weekly active teams. [E2, E5, E6]
