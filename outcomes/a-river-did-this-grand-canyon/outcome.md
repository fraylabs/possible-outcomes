# A River Did This: the Grand Canyon in 57 seconds

A 57-second vertical film of the Grand Canyon being cut, made entirely in code. It opens on "A river did this.", starts on a flat plateau six million years ago, and the canyon deepens as a year counter runs down. Every fact is on screen as words, and it ends on a one-line card. Terrain, water, trees, sky, light, the score and the wind and water sounds were all generated by code; no photo, texture or sample was used.

## How it was made

The Procedural Film Maker skill, run in Claude Code with Claude Opus 5.5, got the brief shown here and nothing else. It wrote a shot list, built the canyon as an eroded height map with ray-marched water and trees and a physical sky, and scored stills of every shot over four rounds with fresh reviewers. It checked the facts against the National Park Service and rendered the film on the same laptop in 58 minutes. Nobody edited the film by hand.

Recorded: made by the Procedural Film Maker skill in a clean folder holding only the skill and a brief, 12 November 2026.

## What to know

- This is below the skill's own bar. Reviewers scored the weakest part of each shot around 41 out of 100 against "indistinguishable from a photo". The rock walls read as smooth clay rather than fractured cliffs. After round 4 gained nothing, the agent stopped and asked whether to render; the person giving the brief said render, labelled below the bar.
- The brief itself said "a mile (1.8 km)". 1.8 km is the deepest point; a mile is 1.6 km. The agent noted the difference, and the person giving the brief had the card changed to "(1.6 km)" before the final render.
- The skill has since been told to stop after two rounds without gain and render without asking, and to check every number and unit on screen.
