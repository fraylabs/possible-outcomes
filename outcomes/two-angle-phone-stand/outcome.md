# Two-Angle Desk Phone Stand

A desk phone stand in two printed parts with no screws or glue: a solid 88 × 88 mm base with two angled sockets, and an L-shaped cradle that drops into either one. The front socket sets a 55° reading angle and the rear socket a 70° video-call angle. A slot through the cradle's ledge and a notch in the base let the charging cable stay plugged in, leaving forward over the desk or backward through a channel underneath. It fits phones 65–80 mm wide and up to 11 mm thick in a case, and both parts print in PLA without supports.

## How it was made

An AI agent made this. Prototype Engineer, Possible's hireable agent running Claude Code with Claude Opus 5.5, got the brief in this recipe and nothing else: a fresh, empty workspace and no follow-ups. It wrote a parametric CadQuery model, then built, checked and rendered it on a remote Linux machine. The final build passed all 50 of its geometry checks: valid solids, no interference in either position, three phone sizes and a charging plug clear of the parts, footprint, bed size and overhangs. It dropped an earlier hinged-kickstand concept after its own statics showed the hinge would lift under a large phone. Possible recorded the session with `possible capture`. Nobody edited the model by hand.

## What to know

This is a digital pre-build only. Nothing has been printed or measured. The tap-stability figures are calculations from an assumed 1 N tap and an assumed desk friction of 0.3; with a solid base the margin near the top of a large phone is thin, and at lower infill a tap there could tip it. The two angles and the plug size are the agent's assumptions, and the report lists the physical tests needed before relying on it. The renders are plain mesh plots, not photorealistic. The brief is public; the agent's standing instructions are not. Results vary between runs.
